# System 13 · Paragraph Organization

**For:** M5/M6 students at Satriwithaya preparing for A-Level English (82) on 14 March 2027, aiming for 80+. Level B2–C1. This system covers the last five items on the paper, 76–80.

## The big idea
In items 76–80 you get four sentences, A to D, and four possible orders. A paragraph is not a pile of sentences. It is a chain, and every sentence except the first holds on to the one before it. Your job is to find the hooks. The 4-step method: **(1)** find the opener, **(2)** find the closer, **(3)** link the middle with pointers and signal words, **(4)** use the options, because they already do half the work.

## Level 1 · Ends

**Finding the opener.** The opener is the one sentence that makes sense with nothing before it. It names the topic in full, defines a term or makes the broad claim. It cannot contain a pointer to something not yet said (*this ability, these benefits, such practices, it, they, the term*) or a connector that answers an earlier sentence (*However, For example, Additionally, Therefore*). *Analogy:* episode 1 of a K-drama introduces the characters by name. A line like "After what she did to him…" belongs to episode 7. *Worked examples:* In TCAS69, "Ideation has become a highly sought-after skill" opens, because the other three sentences lean on it ("this ability", "For example", "Having this ability"). In TCAS66, "While these signals can be so subtle…" sounds big and general, but "these signals" points back, so it cannot open. *Trap:* the topic word sits inside a sentence that cannot open: "Bilingual children, *for instance*, …". Read every sentence to the end.

**Finding the closer.** The closer looks back at the whole paragraph and answers "so what?". It concludes (*Ultimately, Thus, In the long run*), recommends (*should, it's crucial to*) or balances (*both approaches, these benefits and challenges*). *Analogy:* a goal in the 60th minute is not the final whistle. *Worked examples:* TCAS68's introvert item ends "In the long run, embracing one's introverted nature… can foster personal growth". TCAS67's cheating item ends "Therefore, it's crucial for schools and teachers to establish fair and secure exam environments". *Trap:* *As a result* and *Consequently* can sit in the middle of a chain. The closer talks about everything, not only about the sentence just before it.

**Using the four options.** TCAS prints the options in alphabetical order, and they usually split 2+2 by first letter. Decide the opener and two options disappear. Write the two survivors one above the other, find the first place where they differ, and test only that link. *Analogy:* a football group stage, then one knockout match. *Worked examples:* TCAS69 item 76 gave A-B-C-D, A-C-D-B, B-A-C-D and B-D-C-A. B is the opener, so the only question left is whether A or D comes second. In TCAS68 all four options began with C, so you split at place two instead, or you look for a *locked pair* (a pointer that must sit directly after its partner). *Trap:* finding the right opener and choosing the first option that starts with it. TCAS always gives you two options with the correct opener.

## Level 2 · Chains

**Reference chains.** Every pointer must point back to something already on the page, usually in the sentence just before. The most useful pointer is the summary noun, a new label for an old idea: *ideation* becomes "this ability", "least grammatically complex" becomes "this simplicity", and "a system of rationing" becomes "such a system". *Worked examples:* "These benefits" needs at least two benefits before it. "The former… the latter…" needs two things already named, in that order. *Trap:* looking for a repeated word. Match the idea, not the spelling.

**Signal words.** Every signal makes a demand on the sentence before it. *However* needs an opposite idea, *For example* needs a general claim, *Additionally* needs a first point, *Consequently* needs a cause, and *Lastly* needs a list that has already started. *Analogy:* the Skytrain announcement tells you which station you have just left. *Worked examples:* TCAS67's university item runs Initially → additionally → Lastly. TCAS68's Instagram item has "In addition to its visual appeal…", which names the previous point for you. *Trap:* hidden signals in the middle of a sentence, such as "should *additionally* consider", "*also* exert influence" and "has *therefore* been suggested".

**Given → new.** Each sentence starts with something the reader already knows and ends with something new, and the next sentence picks up that new ending. *Analogy:* train carriages, where the hook at the back of one fits the coupling at the front of the next. *Worked examples:* TCAS68's chocolate item goes "…are fermented" → "Following fermentation… revealing the valuable nibs" → "Ultimately, these nibs…". TCAS69's white coat item goes "anxiety" → "This anxiety causes… blood pressure" → "Because the blood pressure reading…". *Trap:* linking two sentences because of a shared word in the middle. Match the END of one sentence to the START of the next.

## Level 3 · Patterns

**Process and chronology.** Each step starts from the product of the step before. *After X*, *Following X*, *Once X* and *Since that time* all name the step that comes just before them. The opener often contains step 1 ("a process that begins with harvesting cacao beans"). In a process, *Ultimately* marks the final step. *Analogy:* making instant noodles, where nobody adds the seasoning before boiling the water. *Worked examples:* "Once the cocoons are complete" needs the worms spinning cocoons first. "Since that time" needs a date first (TCAS66: the 1872 publication). *Trap:* all four options share the opener. Test place two by asking what each time word needs.

**Compare–contrast and classification.** A comparison runs frame → side 1 → side 2 (*on the other hand, by contrast*) → balance (*both approaches*). A classification runs frame ("falls into three major classifications") → groups on a ranked scale, usually climbing from "the most basic" to "the most critical". *Analogy:* a talent show reads the results from third place up to the winner. *Worked examples:* TCAS69's waste item (basic → resource-focused → most critical) and TCAS69's online vs classroom item. *Trap:* opening with the *whereas* sentence because it mentions both sides. It is a detail, not the frame.

**Cause–effect chains and problem–solution.** In a chain, each effect becomes the next cause, like dominoes. In problem–solution, the order is problem → cause → solution → result or recommendation, and the solution must fit the cause that comes before it: "make the alternative cheaper" only makes sense after "farmers burn because it is cheapest". A third shape is definition → example → consequence (TCAS69's fast fashion item). *Trap:* several result words in one set. Follow the dominoes, and put the widest effect or the recommendation last.

## 60-second exam tactic
1. Circle every pointer and connector in all four sentences, including hidden ones.
2. The sentence with no circles is the opener. Cross out two options.
3. Compare the two survivors and test the first place where they differ.
4. Read your order once: the last sentence should look back at the whole paragraph, and no pointer should point forward.

## Quick-fire self-test
1. Can "Bilingual children, for instance, switch languages all day" open a paragraph? **No. "For instance" needs a general claim before it.**
2. "These benefits" needs how many benefits before it? **At least two.**
3. The options are A-C-B-D, A-D-C-B, C-A-D-B and C-B-A-D, and C is the opener. What do you test? **Whether A or B comes second.**
4. In a classification item, where does "The most critical category" usually go? **Last, because the list climbs.**
5. "Following fermentation" must come right after which kind of sentence? **The one in which the beans are fermented.**

## Pep talk
Items 76–80 are the most learnable points on the paper: no rare words, no tricky grammar, just a method and a pencil. Circle the hooks, trust the chain and let the options work for you. That is 6.25 points waiting at the end of the paper, so keep your nerve and collect them.

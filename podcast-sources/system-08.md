# System 08 · World Knowledge: the TCAS70 Radar

**For:** M5/M6 students at Satriwithaya preparing for A-Level English (82) on 14 March 2027, aiming for 80+. Level B2–C1.

## The big idea
TCAS reading passages are built from the news. TCAS69 used stories from mid-to-late 2025; TCAS70 will draw on roughly mid-2025 to late 2026. If you already understand the science and vocabulary behind a topic, you read faster, you spot distractors that twist the facts, and no passage feels alien. This system is a radar for the topics most likely to appear, in three levels: Digital world, Planet and People.

## Level 1 · Digital world

**AI & digital life.** Generative AI does not *know* facts. It *predicts* likely words, pixels or sounds from patterns in its training data. That explains hallucination (a confident answer that is invented or wrong), algorithmic bias (unfair patterns copied from the data) and deepfakes (realistic fake video or audio). Since text-to-video apps such as OpenAI's Sora spread in 2025, commentators have talked about "the end of visual fact". *Analogy:* a quiz-show friend who has read every fan wiki and never says "I don't know". *Worked examples:* "Why do chatbots hallucinate?" The answer is that they predict fluent text but cannot check if it is true, not that they are "designed to trick users". And "bias" in "the hiring software may reflect bias" means an unfair preference, not a personal opinion. *Trap:* options that make AI human, saying it "lied" or "decided". A prediction error is not a lie.

**Social media & online safety.** Free apps earn money from advertising, so algorithms reward engagement: likes, shares, comments and watch time. That produces echo chambers and doomscrolling. Australia's under-16 ban took effect in December 2025. About 4.7 million accounts had been removed by January 2026, yet a regulator report in 2026 found that more than 80% of under-16s were still on social media three months later. France (under-15, from 1 September 2026), Malaysia, Denmark, Greece and the UK are following. *Analogy:* a 7-Eleven that learns your favourite snacks and quietly hides the exit. *Trap:* 4.7 million *accounts* is not 4.7 million *children*. Read the noun after every number.

**Misinformation & media literacy.** The difference is intention. Misinformation is false but shared honestly. Disinformation is false and spread on purpose. Satire is a labelled joke, and it becomes misinformation once someone shares it believing it is real. Fact-checkers read *laterally*: they leave the site and see what other sources say about it. *Trap:* calling every false story "disinformation" when the passage says people shared it by mistake.

## Level 2 · Planet

**Climate & energy.** Greenhouse gases such as CO₂ and methane trap heat, and burning fossil fuels thickens that blanket. Mitigation tackles the cause (renewables, EVs, trees). Adaptation copes with the effects (flood walls, drought-resistant rice). El Niño and La Niña are natural swings in the Pacific. In an El Niño the trade winds weaken and the warm water, and the rain, move east, so Thailand usually gets a hotter, drier dry season. La Niña is roughly the opposite. Forecasters expect a strong, possibly "super", El Niño for 2026–27: a hotter, drier early 2027, drought risk for farmers and a worse PM2.5 season. *Analogy:* the Pacific as a bathtub with a fan pushing warm water to our end. Turn the fan down and the warm water sloshes away. *Trap:* El Niño is not *caused* by climate change, and "usually" does not mean "always".

**Disasters & early warning.** Read every disaster report as cause → warning → response → aftermath. From 24 to 27 September 2026, more than 300 mm of rain fell in 48 hours in parts of Bangkok. The city declared a flood disaster zone on 26 September, and water blocked traffic at 37 locations. The cause was heavy rain plus water flowing south from the North. Warnings went out by *cell broadcast*, which sends one alert from the phone towers to every phone in the area at once, with no phone number or app needed. As of 29 September, about 2.6 million people in 29 provinces had been affected, including all 50 Bangkok districts, and about 940,000 households had been hit. Also remember the Hat Yai floods of late November 2025 (called a "once-in-300-years" event) and the magnitude 7.7 Myanmar earthquake of 28 March 2025, which shook Bangkok. *Analogy:* cell broadcast is the school loudspeaker, while texting is a teacher walking room to room. *Trap:* unit swaps between people, households, provinces and districts.

**Waste, pollution & consumption.** PM2.5 particles are about thirty times thinner than a hair, small enough to reach deep into the lungs. They peak from about December to April, when still air traps them under a warmer layer "like a lid". Fast fashion treats clothes as disposable. In a circular economy, one industry's waste becomes another's resource. Greenwashing makes a brand look greener than it is. *Trap:* "recyclable" means *can* be recycled, not *will* be recycled. Also keep a company's *claim* separate from what the writer *shows*.

## Level 3 · People

**Health & wellbeing.** Teen body clocks shift later because melatonin tends to be released later, and experts recommend 8–10 hours of sleep. "Sedentary" means involving a lot of sitting. Ultra-processed food is *linked to* obesity, but most studies are observational. Thailand is an aged society moving towards a super-aged one, because people live longer and fewer babies are born. *Analogy:* umbrellas are linked to wet roads, but rain causes both. *Trap:* options that upgrade "linked to" into "proves" or "causes".

**Education & future of work.** AI tends to take over *tasks* within a job before whole jobs. To upskill is to get better at your current job. To reskill is to train for a new one. Soft skills and critical thinking gain value. Academic integrity means doing your own work and crediting sources, and changing a few words is not paraphrasing. *Analogy:* levelling up your healer is upskilling, switching her to archer is reskilling, and using someone else's save file is plagiarism. *Trap:* the upskill/reskill swap.

**Culture & communication.** Nonverbal signals depend on culture. The famous "93% nonverbal" figure comes from narrow 1960s experiments on judging feelings. Code-switching means moving between languages in one conversation. Soft power is influence through attraction, as with Thai food, Muay Thai and Lisa. Overtourism is when visitors harm a place: Maya Bay closed from 2018 to 2022 to recover. *Trap:* turning a local meaning ("rude in Thailand") into a universal one.

## 60-second exam tactic
1. When you see the topic, switch on your radar. Recall the one-line science before reading the options.
2. Check the *noun after every number* and the *verb of evidence* (causes / is linked to / may).
3. Reject options that give machines intentions, turn "usually" into "always", or make a local fact global.
4. For vocabulary, look for the near-miss: upskill vs reskill, mitigation vs adaptation, misinformation vs disinformation.

## Quick-fire self-test
1. A grandmother forwards a fake health tip she believes. Misinformation or disinformation? **Misinformation. She had no intention to deceive.**
2. What usually happens to Thailand's dry season in an El Niño year? **It becomes hotter and drier, with a higher drought risk.**
3. How does cell broadcast differ from SMS? **It sends one alert to every phone in an area at the same time, with no phone numbers needed.**
4. A call-centre worker trains as a data analyst. Upskill or reskill? **Reskill.**
5. Why is PM2.5 especially harmful? **It is small enough to travel deep into the lungs, and the finest particles can enter the bloodstream.**

## Pep talk
You don't need to be a scientist to beat these passages. You need a clear picture of how each topic works and the words that come with it. Every news story you read between now and March is free TCAS practice, so read the headline, switch on your radar and ask who, what, why and how we know. See you in System 09!

### Conventions the examiners keep
1. **Options are printed shortest → longest** (by printed length): 95–97% of items within a
   4-character tolerance in TCAS66–69 (64–75% strictly). Numeric option sets go in numeric order.
2. **Paragraph-order option sets** (20 sets, TCAS66–69): the four sequences share two openers (2+2) in 15
   sets, one opener (4×) in 4 sets, and four different openers (the four rotations) in 1 set (69-77). The
   true opener is always one of the shared openers, never the only sequence starting with its letter.
   The sequences are listed alphabetically in 12 of 20 sets: a habit, not a rule.
3. **The key is spread evenly over the paper, not inside parts**: TCAS67 20/19/21/20; TCAS68 20/20/20/20;
   TCAS69 19/21/23/17. The longest run of one key position is 3. Inside a 6-item part one position can be
   the key 50–60% of the time.
4. **Length carries no signal**: the key is the uniquely longest option in 15–27% of items (TCAS67 27%,
   TCAS68–69 16%). Across a set, the key's length rank comes out about a quarter each.
5. **Distractors are plausible and usually grammatical on their own**; they are wrong because of the
   context (the next line, the passage, the tone, one grammar rule). Real papers sometimes slip (8 of 30
   TCAS66–67 text-completion blanks had a form that is wrong anywhere). OUR standard stays strict: every
   distractor must be correct English somewhere.
6. **Negative stems are capitalised**: NOT, EXCEPT, FALSE (and occasionally TRUE).
7. **Stems in reading** are mostly full questions ending in "?" (25/40 and 31/40 in TCAS68–69); the rest
   end in a blank: "The main purpose of the passage is to ________."
8. **Dialogue blanks**: `___(n)___`. The line *after* a blank is the most important clue.
9. **Sources**: TCAS69 printed a source line with publication and date under each text. In OUR mocks every
   reading text gets a dated source line: "Source: adapted from [publication type], [day month year]"
   (real dates for real events), or a clearly fictional outlet labelled as such ("The Bangkok Lantern
   (a fictional newspaper)"); invented data is labelled "illustrative". **Never invent a quotation and
   attribute it to a real, named person or organisation.** Invent named experts (fictional Thai or
   international names, fictional universities are fine: "Dr Pimchanok Wattanasiri, Chao Phraya
   University") or use unnamed roles ("a city spokesperson said").

### Paragraph Organization (TCAS66–69)
4 sentences, opener = the most general sentence (topic or definition; no pronoun or connector at its
start). Chains: *This/These/Such + noun* (this ability, these benefits, this anxiety, such practices,
this phenomenon) · connectors (However, Additionally, For example, Consequently, Initially … Lastly,
Ultimately, Thus, Therefore, In the long run). Patterns: process (chocolate), definition → example →
consequence, classification by importance (waste), compare–contrast (online vs classroom, public vs private
university), cause → effect chain (white-coat syndrome), problem → solution, benefits → challenges →
balance (EVs). Option sets: see Convention 2.

## 2. ITEM-WRITING STANDARD (Chris's six-point check + TCAS realism)

Every multiple-choice item must pass ALL of these:
1. **Natural stem** — sounds like real English a teacher or examiner would write.
2. **Exactly one defensible answer.** Read each distractor against the context: if any could be argued
   right, rewrite it.
3. **The hint (practice items only) must not reveal the answer.** A hint may name *where* to look
   ("the line after the blank", "paragraph 3, the sentence with *however*") or *what job the slot does*
   ("the gap needs a noun after *the*"). It may never give a definition, a synonym of the key, the key
   word itself, or a question whose answer is the key ("Whose grandmother is it?" for a *whose* gap).
   Test: could a student who does not know the skill pick the key from the hint alone? If yes, rewrite.
   `verify.js` fails any hint that contains the key's text.
4. **Length carries no signal.** Print options shortest → longest (ties allowed). Across a set, the key's
   length rank should come out about a quarter each (shortest / 2nd / 3rd / longest); in a mock the key is
   the uniquely longest option in 15–27% of items. Don't make the key the most detailed or most
   "complete-sounding" option by habit, and don't make it never the longest either.
5. **Answer positions balanced over the paper**: 18–22 per position over 80 items, no run of the same
   position longer than 3, no position absent from any 16 consecutive items. Don't force balance inside a
   6-item part. To move a key, change the lengths of the options, then re-sort them.
6. **At least one near-miss distractor**: close to the key but wrong in context (same meaning family,
   wrong tone; right word, wrong form; true fact, wrong paragraph; etc.).

Also:
- Options within one item are **parallel**: same part of speech/grammatical shape (TCAS always does
  this — e.g. `identity / identical / identically / identification`; `that connect / are connected / connect /
  that are connected`).
- No "all of the above / none of the above". No joke options.
- Options have **no HTML** (plain text only). Stems and `why` may use `<em>` and `<strong>`.
- Spelling: TCAS papers use American spelling in most passages; either is fine but be consistent within
  a text.
- British/Thai cultural fit: Thai names (Mint, Pun, Fah, Krit, Ploy, Nan, Bow, Oat, Pim, Tee, Beam, Aom)
  mixed with international names; Thai places; no romance involving minors beyond the innocent
  teen-crush comedy level TCAS uses; nothing sexual, no violence detail, no politics of named Thai
  politicians, no monarchy.
- `why` (explanation): 2–4 sentences in clear B2 English. Say why the key is right (quote the clue) and
  why the tempting distractor(s) fail. This is what students read after answering.

## 3. DATA FORMATS

Files are plain browser JavaScript (no modules, no imports). Use single quotes; escape apostrophes as `\'`
or use the typographic apostrophe ’ in running text. Must parse with `node --check`.

### 3a. Common item fields
```
{ id, type, tag, level, stem, options:[4 strings], answer:<0-based index>, why, hint? }
```
- `tag` — one of the tags in §4 (exact spelling). Pick the tag that names the skill the item tests.
- `level` — 'B1+', 'B2', 'B2+', 'C1', 'C1+'.
- `hint` — practice items only (≤ 20 words, never gives the answer). Mocks: no hint.

### 3b. Item types
| type | fields | use |
|---|---|---|
| `gap` | `lines:[{who,text}]`, `blank:'(n)'`, stem, options, answer | dialogue blank; blanks written `___(n)___` inside `text` |
| `read` | `passage` (plain text; `\n\n` between paragraphs), `source`?, stem, options, answer | reading items |
| `cloze` | `passage` with `___(n)___` blanks, `blank:'(n)'`, stem, options, answer | text completion |
| `choose` | stem (HTML ok), options, answer | general MCQ; paragraph order uses `<div class="orderblock"><p>A. …</p>…</div>` in the stem and options like 'A-C-B-D' |
| `equiv` | `given` (HTML sentence), stem, options, answer | closest in meaning |
| `judge` | `given`, stem, answer (0 True, 1 False, 2 Not given) | practice only |
| `spot` | stem, `words:[segments]`, answer (index of wrong segment), `fix` | practice only (error hunt) |
| `order` | stem, `items:[…in correct order]` | practice only (drag to order) |
| `sort` | stem, `bins:[{key,label,hint}]`, `items:[{text,bin}]` | practice only |
| `build` | stem, `tiles:[…]`, `solution:'…'`, `alt:[…]` | practice only (assemble sentence) |


**Shared context**: when several items share a dialogue/passage/ad/visual, repeat the identical

## 4. THE LADDER — 13 SYSTEMS, 39 LEVELS, 117 SUBS, 117 TAGS

Each sub owns exactly one tag (below). Items in a sub normally use that sub's tag; mocks use these tags
so a wrong answer sends the student to the right lesson. Art keys are fixed.

**t1 · System 01 · Conversation Code** (art `chat`, B1+–C1)
- l1 Everyday reactions — s1 `cv-react` reacting to news (surprise, sympathy, congratulations, relief) · s2 `cv-agree` agreeing, disagreeing & softening · s3 `cv-thanks` thanking, apologising & the right reply to each
- l2 Getting things done — s1 `cv-request` requests, offers, permission, invitations (Would you mind…? Count me in) · s2 `cv-complain` complaints & service recovery (restaurant, hotel, delivery) · s3 `cv-advice` advice, suggestions & warnings
- l3 Reading the turn — s1 `cv-next` the line after the blank decides (question–answer fit, yes/no vs wh-) · s2 `cv-register` who is talking to whom: tone, politeness, relationship · s3 `cv-twist` long conversations: tracking the story, irony & the punchline

**t2 · System 02 · Idioms, Sayings & Discourse Markers** (art `signal`, B2–C1+)
- l1 Idioms & sayings — s1 `id-reaction` reaction idioms (I'm all ears, You can say that again, Count me in, That sounds serious) · s2 `id-situation` situation idioms (play it by ear, stick to the point, draw the line, out of the blue, with flying colours) · s3 `id-proverb` proverbs & sayings (when it rains it pours, every cloud has a silver lining, no pain no gain, actions speak louder…)
- l2 Markers that steer — s1 `dm-add` adding & intensifying (As a matter of fact, Needless to say, On top of that, To make matters worse) · s2 `dm-contrast` contrasting & conceding in talk (On the other hand, Having said that, Mind you, Even so, At any rate) · s3 `dm-rephrase` rephrasing & concluding (In other words, To put it another way, At the end of the day, All things considered)
- l3 Markers that frame — s1 `dm-frame` opening & framing (Without further ado, Now that, By the way, In that case, Speaking of) · s2 `dm-stance` hedging & stance (As far as I know, To be honest, If I'm not mistaken, Apparently, Frankly) · s3 `dm-meta` lecture & argument talk (To make an analogy, To illustrate, To spell it out, to go off on a tangent, To make a distinction)

**t3 · System 03 · Ads & Reviews** (art `megaphone`, B1+–B2+)
- l1 Advertisements — s1 `ad-purpose` purpose & target audience · s2 `ad-detail` selling points, conditions & "NOT mentioned" · s3 `ad-technique` persuasion techniques (urgency, emotional appeal, expert endorsement, bandwagon, logical reason, desire for peace)
- l2 Reviews — s1 `rv-attitude` the reviewer's overall verdict (mixed vs negative vs positive) · s2 `rv-evidence` strengths, drawbacks & the detail that proves them · s3 `rv-infer` reading between the lines in reviews
- l3 Two texts & fine print — s1 `ad-compare` comparing Ad A and Ad B · s2 `ad-fineprint` eligibility, validity, warranty, terms & conditions · s3 `ad-vocab` consumer vocabulary (complimentary, amenities, redeem, subscription, versatile, predecessor)

**t4 · System 04 · News & Articles: Understanding** (art `news`, B2–C1)
- l1 The big picture — s1 `rd-main` main idea & best title · s2 `rd-purpose` purpose of the text / of a paragraph · s3 `rd-news` how news is built (headline, lead, attribution, aftermath questions)
- l2 Finding it — s1 `rd-detail` detail & paraphrase recognition · s2 `rd-notexcept` NOT / EXCEPT / FALSE questions · s3 `rd-reference` reference words (this, those, both groups, it, such)
- l3 Linking ideas — s1 `rd-cause` cause, effect & sequence · s2 `rd-views` who says what: proponents, critics, experts · s3 `rd-support` which detail best supports a claim

**t5 · System 05 · Articles: Thinking** (art `bulb`, B2+–C1+)
- l1 Beyond the words — s1 `rd-infer` inference · s2 `rd-expression` meaning of an expression or sentence in context · s3 `rd-conclude` conclusions & the overall message
- l2 Voice — s1 `rd-tone` tone words · s2 `rd-attitude` the writer's attitude (balanced, critical, fearful, admiring) · s3 `rd-factop` fact vs opinion
- l3 Architecture — s1 `rd-org` organisation patterns · s2 `rd-mention` why the writer mentions X · s3 `rd-next` what comes next, source type & genre

**t6 · System 06 · Visuals & Numbers** (art `chart`, B1+–B2+)
- l1 Tables — s1 `vs-table` reading tables & rankings · s2 `vs-math` arithmetic: times as many, combined, difference, closest pair, approximately · s3 `vs-title` what the visual shows / best title
- l2 Charts — s1 `vs-trend` trend language on line & bar charts (fluctuate, peak, level off, plummet, surge) · s2 `vs-pie` pie charts & parts of a sample · s3 `vs-compare` comparing groups (largest gap, higher than, only group where…)
- l3 Diagrams — s1 `vs-flow` flowcharts & decision paths · s2 `vs-diagram` infographics: sources, components, effects · s3 `vs-trap` NOT provided / EXCEPT and the traps in notes, units & footnotes

**t7 · System 07 · Vocabulary in Context** (art `lexicon`, B2–C1+)
- l1 Clues — s1 `vc-clue` context clues (definition, contrast, example, restatement) · s2 `vc-closest` "closest in meaning / best replaced by" technique · s3 `vc-polysemy` familiar words with exam meanings (trying times, concerned about, thanks to, ill, intake, set in stone)
- l2 Academic words — s1 `vc-verbs` academic verbs (mitigate, enhance, undermine, foster, impede, exacerbate) · s2 `vc-adjs` evaluative adjectives (ubiquitous, vital, sedentary, nefarious, commendable, unprecedented) · s3 `vc-nouns` abstract nouns (upshot, attire, drawback, repercussion, scrutiny, consensus, integrity)
- l3 Word partners — s1 `vc-colloc` collocations (play a role, raise concerns, pose a risk, take into account) · s2 `vc-phrasal` phrasal verbs (bring out, come down with, stem from, rely on, carry out) · s3 `vc-prep` dependent prepositions (focus on, seen as, honest about, benefit from, ban from, through)

**t8 · System 08 · World Knowledge: the TCAS70 Radar** (art `globe`, B2–C1)
- l1 Digital world — s1 `wk-ai` AI & digital life (generative AI, deepfakes, algorithms, hallucination, privacy) · s2 `wk-social` social media & online safety (engagement, echo chambers, under-16 bans, cyberbullying) · s3 `wk-media` misinformation, memes & media literacy
- l2 Planet — s1 `wk-climate` climate & energy (greenhouse gases, net zero, El Niño 2026–27, renewables, EVs) · s2 `wk-disaster` disasters & early warning (flash floods, Thailand floods Sept–Oct 2026, Hat Yai 2025, earthquakes, cell broadcast) · s3 `wk-eco` waste, pollution & consumption (PM2.5, fast fashion, recycling, circular economy)
- l3 People — s1 `wk-health` health & wellbeing (sleep, diet, sedentary life, stress, ageing society) · s2 `wk-work` education & future of work (upskill/reskill, soft skills, AI & jobs, academic integrity) · s3 `wk-culture` culture & communication (nonverbal, intercultural, bilingualism, soft power, generations, overtourism)

**t9 · System 09 · Word Forms & Word Order** (art `layers`, B2–C1)
- l1 Forms — s1 `wf-pos` which part of speech fits the slot · s2 `wf-family` word families & suffixes (identity/identical/identically/identification) · s3 `wf-confuse` confusables (affect/effect, fail/fall, like/alike/likely, rise/raise, economic/economical)
- l2 Phrases — s1 `wo-np` noun phrases & compounds (youth violence, seven-year-olds, today's high-tech world) · s2 `wo-adjorder` adjective order (opinion-size-age-shape-colour-origin-material-purpose) · s3 `wo-adverb` adverb placement & negation (is not simply, may even be, probably feel, truly unusual)
- l3 Tricky pairs — s1 `wf-edIng` -ed/-ing adjectives & linking verbs (look calm, not calmly) · s2 `wf-compare` comparative forms in context (less effectively, the more … the more) · s3 `wo-front` fronting, inversion & emphasis (Not only…, Only when…, Rarely…) — C1 stretch

**t10 · System 10 · Clause Architecture** (art `stack`, B2–C1)
- l1 Relative clauses — s1 `rc-basic` who/which/that/whose/where · s2 `rc-nondef` non-defining & sentence-level which (…, which has caused debates) · s3 `rc-prep` preposition + which/whom (through which, in which, most of whom)
- l2 Shrinking clauses — s1 `rc-reduced` reduced relatives (known as, often described as, the rules introduced last year) · s2 `rc-appos` appositives & commas (a trend, known as the Flynn Effect,) · s3 `ac-reduced` reduced adverbial clauses (when experienced in moderation, if not prepared correctly, while studying)
- l3 Noun clauses — s1 `nc-embedded` embedded questions (how nations use, what the other person says) · s2 `nc-whether` whether/if, whether or not · s3 `nc-it` that-clauses & "It + passive/adjective + that/to" (It has been established that…, It is essential to…)

**t11 · System 11 · Verb Systems** (art `clock`, B2–C1)
- l1 Time — s1 `vt-tense` tense from time signals (since, by the time, for years, recently) · s2 `vt-sva` subject–verb agreement with long subjects (Pressure on students … is prompting) · s3 `vt-aspect` perfect & continuous aspects (have been working, had been, will have)
- l2 Voice — s1 `vp-passive` passive forms in all tenses · s2 `vp-passinf` passive infinitives & gerunds (allow messages to be left; the next foundation to be questioned) · s3 `vp-causative` causatives (have it styled, get something done, make/let)
- l3 Mood & patterns — s1 `vm-subj` the subjunctive (suggest/insist/recommend that … be) · s2 `vm-cond` conditionals: mixed & reduced (hadn't been … might not know; if not prepared) · s3 `vm-pattern` verb patterns (ban/prevent/stop … from -ing; allow/encourage … to; it is essential to)

**t12 · System 12 · Linkers, Determiners & Parallelism** (art `chip`, B2–C1)
- l1 Linkers — s1 `lk-contrast` contrast & concession (although/despite/while/yet/however/unlike/whereas) · s2 `lk-cause` cause, result & purpose (because of/owing to/so that/therefore/thus) · s3 `lk-add` addition & example (as well as, in addition to, moreover, such as/like)
- l2 Determiners — s1 `dt-other` other/another/the other/others/each other · s2 `dt-quant` both/either/neither/every/all/some/each/most · s3 `dt-pron` pronoun reference & agreement (their/its/one's)
- l3 Balance — s1 `pl-parallel` parallel structure · s2 `pl-correl` correlatives (not only … but also; either … or; both … and; neither … nor) · s3 `pl-coord` coordinators (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) & joining sentences correctly

**t13 · System 13 · Paragraph Organization** (art `puzzle`, B2–C1)
- l1 Ends — s1 `po-open` finding the opener · s2 `po-close` finding the closer · s3 `po-options` using the 4 options (the 2+2 or 4× opener split, elimination)
- l2 Chains — s1 `po-ref` reference chains (this/these/such + noun, pronouns) · s2 `po-signal` signal words (However, Additionally, For example, Consequently, Initially, Lastly) · s3 `po-given` given → new information flow
- l3 Patterns — s1 `po-process` process & chronology · s2 `po-compare` compare–contrast & classification · s3 `po-argue` cause–effect chains & problem–solution

- **Thailand floods, Sept–Oct 2026.** Bangkok: more than 300 mm of rain in parts of the city after 48 hours
  of continuous heavy rain (24–27 Sept); the city declared a flood disaster zone at 10:16 on 26 Sept;
  traffic blocked at 37 locations; draining expected to take 2–3 days (Wikipedia, "2026 Bangkok floods").
  DDPM, 29 Sept: 2,602,309 people in 940,300 households *affected*; 29 provinces plus Bangkok flooded
  (42 plus Bangkok since 16 Sept); 23 deaths; about 700,000 people in 329,000 Bangkok households (Nation
  Thailand, 29 Sept). DDPM, 4 Oct: 3,262,676 people in 1,171,165 households; 27 provinces plus Bangkok
  still flooded; **31 deaths**; water falling in Bangkok, rising in the Central region; 702,217 flood-aid
  registrations by 3 Oct (Nation Thailand, 4 Oct). Causes: prolonged heavy rain since 16 Sept plus Chao
  Phraya Dam releases of 1,500–2,500 m³/s. Economy: the Federation of Thai Industries put member losses at
  1.02 billion baht for 24–30 Sept; Honda paused two plants 2–6 Oct; a government disaster-insurance scheme

## 6. VOICE

Write like a sharp, warm teacher who respects teenagers' intelligence (Chris's style: treats students as
intellectually serious; loves first-principles explanations and vivid analogies). No filler, no
"In today's fast-paced world". British spelling in lesson prose; exam passages may use American spelling
as TCAS does.

### Conventions the examiners never break
1. **Options are printed shortest → longest** (by printed length). In TCAS62–69, 85–100% of items obey
   this. Paragraph-order options (all the same length) are in alphabetical order (A-B-C-D, A-C-D-B, B-A-C-D, B-D-C-A).
2. **The key is spread evenly**: TCAS68 key = 20/20/20/20; TCAS69 = 19/21/23/17. The key is the
   longest option only ~14% of the time (TCAS69).
3. **Every distractor is grammatical and plausible on its own.** It is wrong only because of the
   context: it contradicts the next line, the passage, the tone, the relationship, or one grammar rule.
4. **Negative stems are capitalised**: NOT, EXCEPT, FALSE.
5. **Stems in reading** often end with a blank: "The main purpose of the passage is to ________."
6. **Dialogue blanks**: `___(n)___`. The line *after* a blank is the most important clue.
7. **Sources**: real papers print "Source: …". In OUR mocks, write "Source: Adapted for TCAS70 practice"
   or a clearly fictional outlet ("The Bangkok Chronicle"). **Never invent a quotation and attribute it to
   a real, named person or organisation.** Invent named experts (fictional Thai or international names,
   fictional universities are fine: "Dr Pimchanok Wattanasiri, Chao Phraya University") or use unnamed roles
   ("a city spokesperson said").

### Trends TCAS66 → TCAS69 (write TCAS70 one notch harder than 69)
- Options got longer: TCAS69 reading options are full clauses of 60–90 characters.
- Fewer NOT/EXCEPT stems (11/40 in 66–67 → 3/40 in 69); more inference, "which detail best supports",
  expression meaning ("Now that's out the door"), advertising technique, and **arithmetic on visuals**
  ("approximately six times as many", "combined percentage", "percentage difference", "closest pair").
- Vocabulary in context got rarer: *nefarious, upshot, attire, sedentary, intake, vital, set in stone*.
- Conversations moved up to C1 discourse markers and idioms: *Without further ado, Now that, At the end of
  the day, To make an analogy, I'm all ears, When it rains it pours, to make matters worse, play it by ear,
  stick to the point, At any rate*.
- Texts are recent and authentic: TCAS69 used news from July–Oct 2025. **TCAS70 will use news from
  roughly mid-2025 to late 2026.**

### Paragraph Organization (TCAS66–69)
4 sentences, opener fixed by topic sentence (general, no pronoun/connector at its start). Chains:
*This/These/Such + noun* (this ability, these benefits, this anxiety, such practices) · connectors
(However, Additionally, For example, Consequently, Initially … Lastly, Ultimately, Thus, In the long run).
Patterns: process (chocolate), definition→example→consequence, classification by increasing importance
(waste), compare–contrast (online vs classroom, public vs private university), cause→effect chain
(white coat syndrome), problem→solution.

### Reading question types to use (per article of 8, spread like this)
main idea / primary purpose (1) · detail or paraphrase (1–2) · vocabulary in context: "closest in
meaning / can be best replaced by" (1–2) · reference: "the word 'those' refers to" (0–1) · meaning of an
expression or sentence (0–1) · inference / conclusion (1) · organisation of a paragraph or the text (0–1)
· tone / attitude (0–1) · why the author mentions X (0–1) · NOT/EXCEPT (0–1).

## 2. ITEM-WRITING STANDARD (Chris's six-point check + TCAS realism)

Every multiple-choice item must pass ALL of these:
1. **Natural stem** — sounds like real English a teacher or examiner would write.
2. **Exactly one defensible answer.** Read each distractor against the context: if any could be argued
   right, rewrite it.
3. **The hint (practice items only) must not reveal the answer.** A hint may name *where* to look
   ("the line after the blank", "paragraph 3, the sentence with *however*") or *what job the slot does*
   ("the gap needs a noun after *the*"). It may never give a definition, a synonym of the key, the key
   word itself, or a question whose answer is the key ("Whose grandmother is it?" for a *whose* gap).
   Test: could a student who does not know the skill pick the key from the hint alone? If yes, rewrite.
   `verify.js` fails any hint that contains the key's text.
4. **The key is the longest option no more than about 25% of the time.** Vary lengths deliberately: in
   about a quarter of items make the key the SHORTEST option. Don't make the key the most detailed or
   most "complete-sounding" option by habit.
5. **Answer positions balanced.** (A script will reorder options shortest→longest for realism and then
   check balance; you just need to vary which option is longest/shortest.)
6. **At least one near-miss distractor**: close to the key but wrong in context (same meaning family,
   wrong tone; right word, wrong form; true fact, wrong paragraph; etc.).

Also:
- Options within one item are **parallel**: same part of speech/grammatical shape (TCAS always does
  this — e.g. `identity / identical / identically / identification`; `that connect / are connected / connect /
  that are connected`).
- No "all of the above / none of the above". No joke options.
- Options have **no HTML** (plain text only). Stems and `why` may use `<em>` and `<strong>`.
- Spelling: TCAS papers use American spelling in most passages; either is fine but be consistent within
  a text.
- British/Thai cultural fit: Thai names (Mint, Pun, Fah, Krit, Ploy, Nan, Bow, Oat, Pim, Tee, Beam, Aom)
  mixed with international names; Thai places; no romance involving minors beyond the innocent
  teen-crush comedy level TCAS uses; nothing sexual, no violence detail, no politics of named Thai
  politicians, no monarchy.
- `why` (explanation): 2–4 sentences in clear B2 English. Say why the key is right (quote the clue) and
  why the tempting distractor(s) fail. This is what students read after answering.

## 3. DATA FORMATS

Files are plain browser JavaScript (no modules, no imports). Use single quotes; escape apostrophes as `\'`
or use the typographic apostrophe ’ in running text. Must parse with `node --check`.

### 3a. Common item fields
```
{ id, type, tag, level, stem, options:[4 strings], answer:<0-based index>, why, hint? }
```
- `tag` — one of the tags in §4 (exact spelling). Pick the tag that names the skill the item tests.
- `level` — 'B1+', 'B2', 'B2+', 'C1', 'C1+'.
- `hint` — practice items only (≤ 20 words, never gives the answer). Mocks: no hint.

### 3b. Item types
| type | fields | use |
|---|---|---|
| `gap` | `lines:[{who,text}]`, `blank:'(n)'`, stem, options, answer | dialogue blank; blanks written `___(n)___` inside `text` |
| `read` | `passage` (plain text; `\n\n` between paragraphs), `source`?, stem, options, answer | reading items |
| `cloze` | `passage` with `___(n)___` blanks, `blank:'(n)'`, stem, options, answer | text completion |
| `choose` | stem (HTML ok), options, answer | general MCQ; paragraph order uses `<div class="orderblock"><p>A. …</p>…</div>` in the stem and options like 'A-C-B-D' |
| `equiv` | `given` (HTML sentence), stem, options, answer | closest in meaning |
| `judge` | `given`, stem, answer (0 True, 1 False, 2 Not given) | practice only |
| `spot` | stem, `words:[segments]`, answer (index of wrong segment), `fix` | practice only (error hunt) |
| `order` | stem, `items:[…in correct order]` | practice only (drag to order) |
| `sort` | stem, `bins:[{key,label,hint}]`, `items:[{text,bin}]` | practice only |
| `build` | stem, `tiles:[…]`, `solution:'…'`, `alt:[…]` | practice only (assemble sentence) |
